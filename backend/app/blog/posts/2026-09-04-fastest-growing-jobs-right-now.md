You've probably heard that hands-on, blue-collar work is having a moment. It's been in the news for a year.

Here's what the headlines usually skip: the receipts. Which roles, exactly, and by how much — pulled from live job postings this month, not a survey or a hot take.

So we pulled them.

Over the last three months, hiring shrank overall. Across the 2,306 companies we track every week, open jobs fell from 117,783 to 110,221 — down 6.4%, or 7,562 postings gone.

But not every role moved with the tide. Here are the five growing fastest anyway, over the 90 days ending August 31, 2026. One rule: I only counted roles hiring across at least 80 different companies, so a couple of big employers on a spree can't fake a trend.

| Role | Change |
|---|---|
| [Maintenance Technician](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/maintenance-technician) | +17.5% |
| [Electrical Engineer](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/electrical-engineer) | +13.1% |
| [Mechanical Engineer](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/mechanical-engineer) | +8.5% |
| [Sales Representative](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/sales-representative) | +8.1% |
| [Manufacturing Technician](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/manufacturing-technician) | +5.8% |

If you've followed the story, the shape won't surprise you. Not one is a software job. Not one is a design job. These are the people who keep factories running, get hardware built, keep the lights on, and close the deals. What's useful here isn't *that* it's happening — it's exactly which roles, and by how much, right now.

Now look at what slid the other way over the same stretch, in the same companies:

- [Technical Writer](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/technical-writer) — down 24.7%
- [Accountant](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/accountant) — down 21.1%
- [Business Analyst](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/business-analyst) — down 17.1%

The safe, sit-at-a-desk roles — the ones treated as the smart, stable bet — are the ones losing ground fastest.

Even software is easing off. [Software Engineer](https://www.whatsindemand.com/r/software-engineer) is still the single most-hired role we track, with 17,209 openings. But that number is down from three months ago, not up.

One caveat, because it matters. Three months is a snapshot, not a verdict. Some of these roles — maintenance, manufacturing, retail-floor work — tend to staff up heading into fall. So read this as what's hot right now, not a promise about next year.

So treat this as a scoreboard, not a scoop. The trend is old news; the numbers are this week's. And the numbers are what actually help — if you're deciding what to learn, who to hire, or where to point your next move, it pays to know the current score: which roles are adding headcount right now, and which are quietly bleeding it.
